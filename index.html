Why Tenant Representation Matters

Your landlord has
representation. You should too.

When you sign a commercial lease, you're committing to a significant financial and operational decision — often for 3, 5, or 10 years. The landlord's broker is experienced, well-prepared, and working exclusively to protect the landlord's interests. A tenant representative works exclusively for you.

Rental rates & escalations
Negotiating the base rent is only the beginning — annual increases can add significantly to your total cost over the lease term.
Tenant improvement allowances
Build-out costs can be substantial. TI allowances are negotiable, and most tenants leave money on the table.
Operating expenses & lease structure
Gross, NNN, modified gross — what you're actually responsible for under each structure matters as much as the base rent.
Renewal options & flexibility
Early termination rights, expansion options, and renewal terms protect your ability to grow or change course.

Lease Expiring in the Next 12–18 Months?

Most tenants wait too long. Don't.

The window before your lease expires is the most valuable time in any tenant's negotiating position. Landlords know it. Most tenants don't act on it. Starting 12–18 months out gives you the leverage to negotiate renewal terms, explore competing options, and make a deliberate decision — not a rushed one.

Review your current lease terms and identify leverage points
Survey the market for competing spaces — even if you plan to stay
Negotiate renewal rate, TI allowance, and lease structure
No obligation to explore — information is your best tool
