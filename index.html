Why It Matters

Your landlord has representation. You should too.

When you sign a commercial lease, you're committing to a significant financial and operational decision — often for 3, 5, or 10 years. The landlord's broker is experienced and working exclusively to protect the landlord's interests. A tenant representative works exclusively for you.

Rental rates & escalations
Negotiating the base rent is only the beginning — annual increases can add significantly to your total cost over the lease term.
Tenant improvement allowances
Build-out costs can be substantial. TI allowances are negotiable, and most tenants leave money on the table.
Operating expenses & lease structure
Gross, NNN, modified gross — what you're actually responsible for under each structure matters as much as the base rent.
Renewal options & flexibility
Early termination rights, expansion options, and renewal terms protect your ability to grow or change course.
In many commercial leasing transactions, brokerage compensation is structured into the deal — which means working with a tenant representative often costs you nothing directly. We explain how representation and compensation work in every situation before we begin.
